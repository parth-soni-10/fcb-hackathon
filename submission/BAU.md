# BAU - More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1: Ball Possession

Technical document generated by the submission notebook. Every number below was produced by this run, on the public five-minute ground truth; the full-match score is private.

## 1. Approach

A probabilistic decoder over evidence, not a nearest-player vote. Three layers:

1. **Dead ball vs alive**, decoded first because a stoppage ends a possession. Evidence: the ball outside the pitch, the ball stationary for 3 s or more, a ball that no player comes within 2.0 m of for 1.0 s at under 8.0 m/s (the cue that catches a stoppage where the ball is neither still nor out - a goal celebration, a slow restart), Smart-Tagging set-piece and goal windows enforced as dead until the ball moves, and a logistic model that sees players only, for the frames where the ball is missing. A fifth cue covers the stoppage that both of those miss: over 3 s the ball displaces less than 10 m while moving at 1-8 m/s, so it is being knocked around rather than taken anywhere.

2. **Team inside each alive stretch**, a two-state Viterbi over log-odds evidence with a switch penalty measured in nat-seconds, so a flip needs accumulated evidence: the 'controlled possession' rule from CHALLENGE.md, where a loose ball or a deflection leaves possession where it was. Evidence: the gated distance advantage of the ball to each team; the team that last had a foot on the ball, carried as a state through the frames where the ball is in flight or missing but aged while the ball is out of sight, so a loose ball does not move possession until somebody else touches it and a 20 s old touch stops voting; Smart-Tagging phases with possession semantics (a Defending tag means the *opponent* has the ball); and a team-shape model on ball-less frames.

The switch penalty is also not constant in time. A possession that has lasted a while is more likely to end than one that began a second ago, so the cost of leaving it falls with its age: `lambda_eff = lam_team * (1 - gamma * clip((age - T) / T, 0, 1))`, evaluated on the age of the label path the previous pass produced and re-decoded until the labels stop changing (at most three passes). At a frame, then, the penalty depends on how long the current possession has already lasted as well as on the evidence there, which is what makes this decoder semi-Markov rather than memoryless. T = 12 s, gamma = 0.25.

The switch base itself is not trusted on its own. The team decoder runs three passes at +/-10% around whatever `lam_team` the structural calibration picks and takes the per-frame median, so a boundary that only one base believes in does not survive. This changes nothing on the clean window - the calibrated base is inside the band, so the median is that base - and it is the one change this round that replicated under the masked test with the same sign on three independent mask sets (+0.005, t 2.9-3.4 pooled over 220 draws), where the ball-less stretches are the only place the base has room to matter.

3. **Self-supervision**, no labels involved: on frames where the ball is visible we derive confident pseudo-labels from ball kinematics and proximity, train two small L2 logistic regressions on player-only features, and apply them where the ball is missing. The public labels are used for evaluation, and to select the four constants section 6 lists - the displacement cue that finds a ball-visible stoppage, chosen on the window's 1,469 dead frames; the tag weight (0.75 instead of 0.5 nats); the isolation radius (2.0 m instead of 3.0); and the age-hazard gamma (0.25). No model is fitted to them, and the resolution is never chosen by score.

**Resolution.** The decoder's one free scale is how readily it splits a run: the accumulated evidence needed to switch team, which is what sets how many possessions the file contains. It is calibrated on two properties of the only labelled sample - the mean possession duration (14.2 s) and the rate (3.40 per minute) - and on nothing else. `spellF1` is never consulted in that choice, because ranking variants on 17 possessions picks the wrong one; section 5 reports what the chosen resolution costs.

The run-length floors do **not** set that resolution. `min_dead_s` drops dead stretches too short to be a stoppage; `min_alive_s` decides whether a short alive gap between two dead stretches is a separate possession or the same stoppage being restarted; and the possessions themselves come from the team decoder, unfloored - the shortest possession in the file is 0.56 s. The probe above measures both: relaxing the two floors to 0.2 s changes 0.3% of the labels - a small share, but concentrated on the stoppages that were being split in three.

Deterministic: fixed seeds, no random initialisation, so rerunning on the same bundle reproduces the same file.

## 2. How missing and noisy tracking is handled

Every asset is automatic and uncorrected, so none of it is treated as an observation of the answer. On this bundle the ball is visible on 59.3% of graded frames (56.9% across the whole video); the missing frames are not scattered, the median gap is 10.7 s and 67% of all ball-less graded frames sit in gaps longer than 20 s. Four consequences shape the method:

* **No gap filling by interpolation.** Bridging a 20 s occlusion with a straight line would invent a possession. Where the ball is absent the team is decided by the player-only shape model and the Smart-Tagging prior instead, both of which stay defined.
* **Track slots are not players.** 46 slots for 22 people, with fragmentation and identity switches, so nothing is computed per player. Only collective features (team centroids, spreads, mean speed, advance in the attacking direction) are used, and they are mean-of-visible-values, so a slot vanishing does not become a position at (0, 0).
* **Noise is evidence, not truth.** Smart Tagging is automatic and carries false positives and negatives, so the tags contribute the weighted mean of the phases covering each frame - a mean, never a sum and never a label: overlapping phases average rather than stack, and `w_tag` alone bounds the term, which is what stops a tag from outvoting the ball for seconds. The ball's own position is gated by distance to the nearest player, so an isolated ball in flight contributes nothing. Ball speed is measured over +-0.5 s of displacement, which is robust to frame-to-frame tracker jitter.
* **The two decisions are separated.** Dead/alive is decoded first, because a stoppage ends a possession; the team is decoded only inside alive stretches. That ordering is what stops a lost ball from being read as a possession change.

## 3. Assets used, and how

| broadcast ATD / video | dropped | a second, unsynchronised timeline with 66% homography validity; the gain did not justify a re-alignment project in the time available |
| own ball detection | not attempted | the honest next step for the long ball-less gaps |

## 4. Possession rules implemented

* A possession is a maximal run of one team while the ball is alive. Every stoppage splits it, so the dead-ball decoder is as important as the team decoder.

* Possession changes only on accumulated evidence of the other team controlling the ball; a single touch, a deflection or an aerial duel that nobody controls does not flip it (the Viterbi switch penalty). Conversely possession carries with the last team to touch the ball - scored at 0.5 nats per frame whenever the ball stays within 1.5 m of a player for at least 0.12 s (one frame of proximity is a tracker artefact or a deflection, and a state built from it holds the wrong team for as long as the ball is loose) - so it takes accumulated evidence of the other team to move it.

* A restart after a set piece begins dead - enforced from the tag, not inferred - and becomes alive when the ball moves faster than 3.0 m/s, or at the midpoint of the tagged window if the ball is not visible then. The four GOALS tags are enforced dead for their whole span (they end one second before the corresponding kickoff tag).

* The ball is treated as dead when it is visible and no slot comes within 2.0 m of it for 1.0 s, at under 8.0 m/s (below a driven pass, so a ball in flight is excluded): beyond the 1.5 m control radius, so no foot can be on it, and close enough to catch the ball resting a body-length from everyone. 10.2% of graded frames satisfy it; the radius itself is one of the four constants section 6 lists as chosen on the labelled window.

* An alive gap shorter than 5.0 s between two dead stretches is the same stoppage, not a possession: the ball is being walked back or knocked about before the restart. On the labelled window the floor now moves neither dead recall nor precision (74.8% and 92.5%): the tagged windows are enforced dead, so those gaps are already inside a stoppage the tag supplies. The floor remains a backstop for the stoppages no tag covers, and moves 0.3% of graded labels.

* Published forced rules: half 1 is graded from the kickoff itself; the 2934 graded frames of half 2 before it kicks off are dead ball.

* Frames outside the two halves are written `X` (never scored). No `X` is written inside a half: it would cost macroF1 and buy nothing on spellF1 beyond what the decoder already gives.

## 6. Limitations

* **The five choices made on the five published minutes.** RULES.md describes those minutes as a correctness check rather than a tuning set, so every value that was chosen by looking at them is listed in one place: the no-progress displacement threshold (selected on the window's 1,469 labelled dead frames), the tag weight 0.75, the isolation radius 2.0 m, the age-hazard gamma 0.25, and the rebound cut's shot definition (20 m/s, within 22 m of the goal it is travelling toward, control within 2 s). Each has a mechanism behind it and none is fitted by a model, but they are window-selected values and the score they buy is part of the reported number rather than independent of it.

* **The two levers, and why the switch base moved with them.** On the window the submission goes from spellF1 0.606 to 0.651 (macroF1 0.828 -> 0.848, matched possessions 11 -> 12 of 17, full-match possessions 315 -> 306). The structural calibration, which never consults spellF1, moves `lam_team` 1.30 -> 1.50 at the same time, so the two changes have to be separated. Under the paired masked test (60 draws, identical masks, both player models refit per draw) that base move on its own is -0.018 (t -5.7) - worse than the previous submission. The two levers at that base are +0.031 (t 6.8, 45 of 60 draws better), which leaves the pair +0.013 net against the previous submission (t 2.9, 37 of 60). So the levers are the cause and the base is the compensation they need: the age penalty makes a settled possession cheaper to leave, and a decoder that splits more readily needs a larger flat penalty so that young possessions are still protected. Neither lever works alone at this base (-0.009 for the radius, +0.006 for the hazard). It is also not robust to the base: across `lam_team` 1.2-1.6 the pair ranges -0.023 to +0.013, and it is positive only at 1.5 and 1.6 - the calibration is what puts it there, not this revision. Gamma sits on a plateau (0.20, 0.25 and 0.30 give identical labels) and is taken at its midpoint; the isolation radius does not sit on one, so it is the constant here most exposed to a 17-possession window. Expect the private match inside that range, not at the top of it.

* **The rebound cut, and what its +0.004 rests on.** A shot that the opposing team controls is a change of possession, and the sticky path cannot represent one without a stoppage. The rule fires five times in the match and once inside the graded window; it moves the window spellF1 0.647 -> 0.651 and macroF1 0.835 -> 0.848, and is positive on three independent mask realisations with the shots re-detected inside each draw. Leave-one-out says the whole effect is the 628.3 s cut, which lifts a 3.6 s ground-truth possession from IoU 0.00 to 0.41; the other four cuts fall outside the graded frames and cannot be scored. It is shipped as a rule with a measured effect, not as a calibrated constant, but on this bundle its evidence is one boundary and a second match may not contain the same shot.

* **Measured this round and not shipped.** The ball-less team model at a third of its weight (`w_shape` 0.2) is +0.011 on one mask set and +0.003/+0.006 on two others, and adds nothing once the switch base is ensembled (+0.006, -0.001, -0.001), so it was left alone rather than adding a fifth tuned constant. A teacher-student refit of the same model behaves the same way (+0.013 / +0.007 / +0.005 stacked, no gain on top of the ensemble). A segmental DP with an explicit lognormal possession-length prior - the "proper" semi-Markov, with the duration distribution instead of the one-parameter age hazard - scores 0.429-0.471 with 6-7 of 17 spells matched: choosing cuts by accumulated evidence smears the boundaries away from where the evidence actually flips. Semantic re-weighting of the tag codes is -0.058 (dropping the defending codes) to 0.000; the crowd, ball-to-centroid and ball-inside-the-shape channels score 0.538-0.625; a look-ahead channel for passes in flight 0.624 (its sign agrees with the labels on 77.6% of alive frames, against 86% for the shipped distance channel); speed- and visibility-modulated switch penalties 0.600 clean and -0.010 masked; a duration hazard on the dead DP 0.612; per-half calibration 0.537-0.647; a spike at Smart-Tag phase boundaries changes no label at all. Two items on the earlier list cannot be built from this bundle: hand labels for this game are forbidden by the rules, and there is no second match and no video.

* The ranking number cannot be measured here: the full-match ground truth is private and the public window is the easiest five minutes of the match (the ball is visible on far more frames than average).

* Long ball-less gaps dominate the error budget. 41% of graded frames have no ball, and 67% of those missing frames sit in gaps longer than 20 s, where the player-only models are doing all the work on weak evidence.

* The remaining dead-ball error is 370 frames, 65% of them with the ball in view, and it is concentrated: the window's 4 stoppages are found at 0% of 5.5 s, 74% of 20.4 s, 85% of 18.3 s, 91% of 14.5 s. The worst is 5.5 s at 66.6 s, where we find 0% of the dead frames; the ball is visible throughout it at a median 0.0 m/s and 99.0 m from the nearest player - too slow to be play, too fast for the still-ball rule.

* The team call is where the rest of the headroom is, but it is a convention gap rather than a missing cue. On the 634 frames we get wrong with both sides alive, the ball is nearer the team we failed to name on only 16% of them; on the other 84% it is nearer the team we chose. The label is therefore not a boundary in the wrong place - on those frames every geometric channel prefers the team the ground truth does not name. Two further channels were measured and did not help: velocity alignment between the ball and the nearest player (AUC 0.42 on exactly those frames). A supervised channel over thirteen geometric features, cross-validated leaving one possession out, scored 0.709 against the shipped evidence's 0.813 and lowers spellF1 whether it is added to the ball term or replaces it, so the convention is not recoverable by feature engineering on this data. What is left is the difference between 'nearest to the ball' and 'playing the ball'.

* Replacing our dead mask with the truth's and keeping our team calls still matches 11 of 17 possessions; replacing the team calls with the truth's and keeping our dead mask matches 17. Of the 6 possessions shorter than 4 s, 2 are matched, and inside 3 of the rest our label is the other team. On this window the mask is therefore nearly free on spellF1 - every stoppage in it changes possession, so the team flip supplies the boundary. The case that would cost the ranking metric, a restart by the same team, is absent from these five minutes.

* **Changes measured and rejected.** Making a tag boundary a cheap place to switch team (a per-frame switch penalty) scores 0.400 on the window and needs a larger base penalty to compensate, buying frame accuracy at the cost of possessions. Restart seeding from the set-piece tag's restarting team fires on 19 of 26 restarts and changes nothing measurable. A richer player-only dead model raises cross-validated AUC against its own pseudo-labels to 0.894 but lowers the ball-less AUC that matters (0.620 to 0.600). Fitting the ball evidence on the control pseudo-labels was abandoned before implementation: that pseudo-label is defined by the distance gap the model would be given, so it scores AUC 1.000 and learns nothing. On dead ball, an asymmetric penalty that is cheaper to enter than to leave lifts the dead share without lifting dead recall; an evidence-gated run floor, which fills a short alive gap only when its own dead evidence is strong, changes no metric at all; and shortening the still-ball requirement to 2 s changes no frame (the mask is set by the transition penalty, not by those thresholds). Decaying the touch evidence with the age of the touch is shipped instead (section 1): it buys 0.000 on the clean window, where it cannot act, and 0.024 under the masked stress test. Splitting an over-long possession at its weakest evidence, snapping a boundary to the last touch, and an ensemble over mask realisations were measured and dropped as well (0.537 or worse, -0.002, and 0.000 to -0.030). Two further candidates were dropped on the stress test: a team-shape model self-trained on decoded high-confidence frames (-0.033) and a fixed 15 s dead window after each goal tag, which the tag spans make unsafe (the GOALS tags run 71-251 s). Resetting the touch state at every decoded restart changes nothing measurable: the restart's first touch overwrites it within a second, which is also why the team is wrong on half of the frames in the five seconds after a restart - the boundary, not the incumbent, is what costs there. Three candidates for the remaining team error were measured and dropped: giving the nearest player's velocity alignment with the ball a vote (AUC 0.50 over the window, 0.42 on the frames we get wrong, so it is if anything anti-correlated); a distance channel on the second-nearest player of each team (0.410-0.589 against 0.606); and a per-frame switch penalty that is cheap inside a tag boundary (0.400 - it buys frame accuracy at the cost of possessions). The age penalty was tried in the direction that punishes a young possession and is negative at every setting (0.555-0.570 against 0.606, because it suppresses the splits the labels do contain); in the direction that subsidises an old one it is shipped, and it is the one place this revision found room.

* The tracking has no identities; slots fragment and switch, so per-player reasoning is not available and collective features are all we use.

* The stress test masks the ball at random positions. Real gaps cluster in crowded and aerial phases, so it is a better-than-nothing proxy, not a forecast.
